## What economics studies

**Economics** studies how people, firms and governments choose what to do with limited resources.

A **resource** is anything used to make something people want: time, money, land, machines, workers' skills.

**Scarcity** means the resources are not enough to satisfy every want. You have 24 hours a day and one salary. A country has a fixed number of workers this year. Because of scarcity, every use of a resource is a choice, and every choice rules out other uses.

## Opportunity cost

The **opportunity cost** of a choice is the value of the best alternative you gave up.

Example: you spend Saturday on a ₹2,000 freelance job instead of a free cricket match you value at ₹1,200, or a ₹1,500 tutoring gig. The opportunity cost of the freelance job is the tutoring gig (₹1,500), the best thing you dropped. You do not add up all the options you skipped, only the best one.

Opportunity cost includes money and things that are not money, like time and enjoyment.

## Thinking at the margin

**Marginal** means one more unit: one more hour, one more cup, one more worker.

- **Marginal benefit**: the extra benefit from one more unit.
- **Marginal cost**: the extra cost of one more unit.

The rule: keep doing something while the marginal benefit is at least the marginal cost, and stop when it falls below.

Example: the 1st hour of study raises your score by 15 marks, the 4th by 3. If an hour of your time is worth 5 marks to you, study 3 hours and stop.

## Incentives and sunk costs

An **incentive** is a reward or penalty that changes the benefit or cost of a choice. Prices, fines, tax breaks and bonuses are all incentives. When an incentive changes, people's choices change, often in ways the designer did not plan.

A **sunk cost** is money or time already spent that you cannot get back. It is the same whichever option you pick next, so it should not affect the decision. A non-refundable ₹500 movie ticket does not oblige you to sit through a bad film. Only future costs and benefits count.
