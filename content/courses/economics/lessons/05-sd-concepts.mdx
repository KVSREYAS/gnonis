## Markets and demand

A **market** is any setup where buyers and sellers trade a good: a vegetable bazaar, an app store, the job market.

**Quantity demanded** is how much buyers want to buy at a given price. The **demand curve** plots it for every price. It slopes down: when the price rises, people buy less, because some switch to other goods and some simply cannot afford as much.

This pattern is called the **law of demand**.

## Supply

**Quantity supplied** is how much sellers want to sell at a given price. The **supply curve** slopes up: a higher price makes it worth producing more, and draws in sellers whose costs are higher.

Each point on the supply curve says "at ₹X, sellers will offer Y units". Each point on the demand curve says "at ₹X, buyers will take Y units".

## Equilibrium, shortage and surplus

The **equilibrium price** is where quantity demanded equals quantity supplied. At that price the market **clears**: everyone who wants to buy at that price can, and every seller who wants to sell can.

- Price **below** equilibrium: buyers want more than sellers offer. That gap is a **shortage**. Queues form and sellers raise prices.
- Price **above** equilibrium: sellers offer more than buyers want. That gap is a **surplus**. Stock piles up and sellers cut prices.

## Shifts versus movements

Two kinds of change get mixed up:

- A **change in price** moves you **along** a curve. It changes quantity demanded or supplied.
- A change in anything else **shifts** the whole curve. For demand: income, tastes, prices of related goods, number of buyers. For supply: input costs, technology, number of sellers, weather.

A **substitute** is a good used instead (tea and coffee). A **complement** is used together (cars and petrol). If coffee gets dearer, demand for tea shifts right.
