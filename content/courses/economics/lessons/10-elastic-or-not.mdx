## How to play

Each card shows a price change and what happened to the quantity sold. Call it **elastic** (buyers reacted more than the price moved) or **inelastic** (less). The card then shows the midpoint elasticity and why.

You can do the maths, but try calling it from the type of good first. That is the skill that sticks.

## What to remember

The same good is usually more elastic in the long run than in the short run. Petrol is the classic case: next week you still drive to work, but over years you can buy a more efficient car or move closer. A review of fuel studies found an average elasticity of about 0.25 within a year and about 0.64 in the long run (Goodwin, Dargay and Hanly, 2004). So petrol gets much more elastic over time, but it usually stays below 1.

A whole category (any soft drink) is less elastic than one brand inside it (one cola), because brands substitute for each other.
