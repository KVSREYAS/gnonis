## Elasticity changes along a line

On a straight-line demand curve the slope is constant, but elasticity is not. At high prices and low quantities, a ₹1 change is a small % of price and a big % of quantity, so demand is elastic. Near the bottom it is the reverse.

Revenue rises as you cut price in the elastic part and falls in the inelastic part. It peaks exactly in the middle, where elasticity = 1.

## Why it matters

A seller in the inelastic part of demand is leaving money on the table: raising the price brings in more revenue and sells fewer units, so costs fall too. That is why a firm with pricing power never chooses a price in the inelastic part of its demand curve. You will use this in the monopoly lesson.
