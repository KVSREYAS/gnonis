## Kinds of cost

- **Fixed cost**: paid no matter how much you make, like rent for a shop.
- **Variable cost**: grows with output, like ingredients.
- **Total cost** = fixed + variable.
- **Average cost** = total cost ÷ units.
- **Marginal cost (MC)**: the extra cost of making one more unit.

In the short run fixed costs are already committed, so whether to make one more unit depends only on marginal cost.

## Marginal revenue and the MR = MC rule

**Marginal revenue (MR)** is the extra revenue from selling one more unit.

The rule: keep producing while MR is above MC. Stop where they are equal. Past that point each extra unit costs more than it brings in.

This rule works for every firm. What changes between market types is what MR looks like.

## Perfect competition

**Perfect competition** means many sellers of an identical product, buyers who know all the prices, and free entry and exit. No single seller can change the market price, so they are **price takers**. For them, MR = price.

So each firm produces where price = MC. If firms earn profits, new firms enter, supply rises and price falls. In the long run price is pushed down to the lowest average cost, and profit beyond a normal return goes to zero.

## Monopoly

A **monopoly** is the only seller of a good with no close substitute. It faces the whole downward demand curve, so to sell one more unit it must cut the price on every unit. That makes MR lower than price.

The monopolist sets MR = MC, which means a lower quantity and a higher price than competition would give. The trades that would have happened between those two quantities are lost: the **deadweight loss**.
