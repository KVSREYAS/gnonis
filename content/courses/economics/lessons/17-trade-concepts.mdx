## Two kinds of advantage

- **Absolute advantage**: you can make more of a good with the same time or resources.
- **Comparative advantage**: you can make a good at a **lower opportunity cost**, meaning you give up less of other goods to make it.

A person can have the absolute advantage in everything, but not the comparative advantage in everything. Opportunity costs are ratios, and if one ratio is lower for you, the other must be lower for the other person.

## Worked example

Asha makes 4 kg rice or 2 m cloth per hour. Ben makes 1 kg rice or 3 m cloth per hour.

- Asha's cost of 1 kg rice: 0.5 m cloth. Ben's: 3 m cloth. Asha has the comparative advantage in rice.
- Asha's cost of 1 m cloth: 2 kg rice. Ben's: 0.33 kg. Ben has the comparative advantage in cloth.

![What Asha and Ben can make alone in 10 hours](/diagrams/economics/ppf.png)

The lines show every mix each can make alone. This is called a **production possibilities frontier**.

## Terms of trade

The **terms of trade** is the price of one good in units of the other. Trade helps both people if the price is between their opportunity costs.

Here rice costs Asha 0.5 m cloth and Ben 3 m. At any price between 0.5 and 3 m cloth per kg, Asha gets more cloth for her rice than she could make herself, and Ben gets rice cheaper than making it.
