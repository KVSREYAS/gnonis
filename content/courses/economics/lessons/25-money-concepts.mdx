## Inflation and price indexes

**Inflation** is the rate at which the general level of prices rises, usually per year. If inflation is 5%, a basket that cost ₹1,000 last year costs ₹1,050 now.

It is measured with a **price index**: the cost of a fixed basket of goods relative to a base year. India's main measure is the **Consumer Price Index (CPI)**, published monthly by the National Statistics Office. The RBI's target is 4% CPI inflation, with a band of 2% to 6%.

## Nominal and real

- **Nominal**: measured in the rupees of the time. Your salary slip is nominal.
- **Real**: adjusted for inflation, measured in rupees of a fixed year, so it shows what the money can buy.

If your salary rises 8% and inflation is 6%, your real salary rose about 2%.

The same goes for interest. The **real interest rate** ≈ nominal rate − inflation. A 7% fixed deposit with 6% inflation earns about 1% in real terms, before tax.

## Compound interest

**Simple interest** pays only on the original amount. **Compound interest** pays on the original plus all interest already earned, so growth speeds up over time.

After n years at rate r, ₹1 becomes (1 + r)ⁿ.

The **rule of 72**: money doubles in about 72 ÷ r years. At 8% that is 9 years, at 12% about 6 years. Over long periods small differences in r make huge differences, and so does starting early.
