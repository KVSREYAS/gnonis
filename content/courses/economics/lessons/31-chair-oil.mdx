## The hard case

A **supply shock** is a sudden change in production costs, such as an oil price spike. It raises inflation **and** slows the economy at the same time. This mix is called **stagflation**.

No rate setting fixes both. Raise rates and the slowdown gets worse. Hold rates down and inflation feeds into expectations and stays high. The 1970s oil shocks went badly partly because central banks let expectations drift up.

## Takeaway

Central banks often "look through" a one-off supply shock if expectations stay anchored, and act firmly if inflation starts feeding into wages and expected prices. Credibility built in calm years is what makes that possible.
