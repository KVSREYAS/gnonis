## How this course was checked

Each fact, date and name in this course was checked in November 2026 against the sources on this page. Every worked answer and game hint was recomputed. The full list of what changed is in docs/econ-factcheck.md in the repo.

Prices like ₹22 lemonade or ₹60 ferries are made-up examples for practice, not real data.

## Textbooks

- CORE Econ, *The Economy 2.0* (free online): [core-econ.org/the-economy](https://www.core-econ.org/the-economy/)
- OpenStax, *Principles of Economics 3e* (free): [openstax.org](https://openstax.org/details/books/principles-economics-3e)
- N. Gregory Mankiw, *Principles of Economics* (Cengage), chapters on supply and demand, elasticity, taxes, externalities and monopoly.
- David Ricardo, *On the Principles of Political Economy and Taxation* (1817), chapter 7: [econlib.org](https://www.econlib.org/library/Ricardo/ricP.html)

## Papers and data

- Akerlof (1970), The market for "lemons": [doi.org/10.2307/1879431](https://doi.org/10.2307/1879431)
- Hardin (1968), The tragedy of the commons: [doi.org/10.1126/science.162.3859.1243](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.162.3859.1243)
- Ostrom, Nobel Prize 2009: [nobelprize.org](https://www.nobelprize.org/prizes/economic-sciences/2009/ostrom/facts/)
- Goodwin, Dargay and Hanly (2004), fuel price elasticities: [doi.org/10.1080/0144164042000181725](https://doi.org/10.1080/0144164042000181725)
- Friedman (1968), The role of monetary policy: [jstor.org/stable/1831652](https://www.jstor.org/stable/1831652)
- India keeps the 4% (2-6%) inflation target for 2026-2031, March 2026: [newindianexpress.com](https://www.newindianexpress.com/business/2026/Mar/25/centre-retains-inflation-target-at-4-for-another-five-years)
