## What game theory studies

**Game theory** is the study of decisions where your result depends on what other people choose, and theirs depends on you. It covers chess, but also pricing wars, auctions, voting, wage talks and evolution.

Every game has three parts:

- **Players**: who is deciding.
- **Strategies**: the options each player can choose. A **strategy profile** is one choice for every player.
- **Payoffs**: the number each player gets for each strategy profile. Bigger is better.

## The payoff matrix

When players move at the same time, or without seeing each other's move, we draw a **payoff matrix**. Rows are your strategies, columns are the other player's. Each cell holds two numbers: **your payoff first** (gold in this course), **theirs second** (green).

![The prisoner's dilemma written as a payoff matrix](/diagrams/game-theory/prisoners-dilemma.png)

Here both payoffs are years in prison written as negative numbers, so −1 is better than −5.

## Trees and kinds of games

When players move in turns and see earlier moves, we draw a **game tree**: each node is a decision, each branch a move, each end point a list of payoffs.

Two more words:

- **Zero-sum**: the payoffs in every cell add to the same total, so one side's gain is the other's loss. Chess and penalty kicks are zero-sum. Trade is not.
- **Rational**: a player who always picks the option with the highest payoff for themselves, given what they believe. Game theory starts by assuming this, then tests it against real behaviour.
