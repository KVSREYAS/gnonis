## The game

Everyone picks a whole number from 0 to 100. The winner is whoever is closest to **2/3 of the average** of all picks.

John Maynard Keynes used a newspaper beauty contest in 1936 to describe stock markets: you win not by picking what you like, but what you think others will pick. Rosemarie Nagel ran this number version as a lab experiment in 1995.

## Levels of thinking

- **Level 0** picks at random, averaging 50.
- **Level 1** expects level 0 and picks 2/3 × 50 ≈ 33.
- **Level 2** expects level 1 and picks 2/3 × 33 ≈ 22.
- Repeat forever and you reach **0**, the only Nash equilibrium.

![Level 0 at 50, level 1 at 33, level 2 at 22, equilibrium at 0](/diagrams/game-theory/beauty-levels.png)

In Nagel's experiment the first-round average was about 37, and nobody picked 0. Picking 0 in round 1 loses.

## The lesson

Game theory's answer (0) is right only if everyone is fully rational and everyone knows that everyone is. When that fails, the best play is to model the people you are actually playing against. In repeated rounds the crowd learns, and guesses fall toward 0, just as Nagel's data showed.
