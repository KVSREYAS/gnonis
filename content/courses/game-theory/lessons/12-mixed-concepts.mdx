## Pure and mixed

A **pure strategy** is one definite choice. A **mixed strategy** picks among choices with fixed probabilities, like "left 40%, right 60%".

The **expected payoff** of a mix is the average payoff you would get over many plays: each payoff times its probability, added up.

Example: score 0.9 of the time if you go left, 0.6 if you go right, mixing 50/50 gives 0.5 × 0.9 + 0.5 × 0.6 = 0.75.

## The indifference rule

In matching pennies, any predictable pattern can be exploited. If you play heads 70% of the time, the other player always plays tails.

In a **mixed-strategy equilibrium**, each player mixes so that the **other** player gets the same expected payoff from every option they use. Then the other player has no better reply, so they are happy to keep mixing too.

This sounds backwards: your mix is set by **their** payoffs, not yours.

## Penalty kicks

A penalty kick is a real zero-sum game. Ignacio Palacios-Huerta studied 1,417 professional penalties (2003) and found scoring rates near these:

| | Keeper left | Keeper right |
|---|---|---|
| Kick left | 58% | 95% |
| Kick right | 93% | 70% |

![The keeper's dive mix that makes both sides equally good for the kicker](/diagrams/game-theory/penalty-indifference.png)

The keeper should dive left about 42% of the time, making both kick directions score about 80%. Kickers' and keepers' real choices were close to the equilibrium mixes.
