## The game

John Maynard Smith and George Price (1973) used game theory for animal conflict. Animals compete for a resource worth **V**.

- **Hawk** always fights. Two hawks fight: one wins V, the other pays the injury cost **C**. On average each gets (V − C)/2.
- **Dove** displays and backs off. Hawk vs dove: hawk takes V, dove gets 0. Two doves share: V/2 each.

Strategies that earn more than average spread through the population over generations.

## The stable mix

If C is bigger than V, a population of all hawks fights itself to pieces, so doves do better and grow. A population of all doves is easy to exploit, so hawks grow. The population settles where both earn the same: a share of hawks equal to **V/C**.

![Starting from 5% or 95% hawks, the share converges to V/C = 40%](/diagrams/game-theory/hawk-dove.png)

That stable mix is an **evolutionarily stable strategy**: a small group of mutants playing differently cannot do better.

## Takeaway

Mixed equilibria can describe a population rather than one random player: 30% of the animals play hawk, 70% dove. The same idea is used for strategies in markets and for which behaviours survive in a group.
