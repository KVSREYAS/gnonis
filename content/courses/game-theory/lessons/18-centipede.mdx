## The game

A pot starts at 4 coins and doubles each time a player passes. On your turn you **take** (you get 3/4 of the pot, the other gets 1/4) or **pass**. You move first, then the bot, alternating, for 6 stages.

Backward induction: at the last stage the mover takes. Knowing that, the one before takes. All the way back to stage 1: take immediately and get 3. Yet in experiments (McKelvey and Palfrey, 1992) most people pass a few times.

## Theory and people

Backward induction assumes everyone is perfectly rational and everyone knows it, all the way down. One small doubt ("maybe the other player will pass") makes passing worthwhile early on.

The lesson is not that the theory is wrong. It shows exactly how much trust or knowledge you need for cooperation to pay, and what to do when you know how the other side really plays.
