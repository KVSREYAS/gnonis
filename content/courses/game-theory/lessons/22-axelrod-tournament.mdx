## Round one: no mistakes

You pick one strategy to enter. It plays every other entry (and a copy of itself) for 50 rounds. The field is a mix of nice, nasty and random players. Finish in the top 2.

## Round two: 5% noise

Now each move flips by mistake 5% of the time. One accidental defection can set off a chain of retaliation between two tit-for-tat players that lasts until another mistake breaks it. Grim trigger never recovers at all.

Strategies that forgive some defections, or that return to cooperating after mutual defection, tend to do better here. Results vary from run to run, so you may need a retry.

## Takeaway

Axelrod's summary of what does well: be **nice** (never defect first), **retaliate** (do not let defection pay), **forgive** (go back to cooperating), and be **clear** (easy for others to read). In a noisy world, extra forgiveness matters more.
