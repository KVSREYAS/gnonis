## How this course was checked

Each fact, date and name in this course was checked in November 2026 against the sources on this page. Every equilibrium, payoff and game hint was recomputed, and the game configs were simulated. The full list of what changed is in docs/econ-factcheck.md in the repo.

The bots in the games are invented. Their habits are set to make good puzzles, not to copy real people.

## Books

- Martin J. Osborne, *An Introduction to Game Theory* (Oxford, 2004): [economics.utoronto.ca/osborne/igt](https://www.economics.utoronto.ca/osborne/igt/)
- Avinash Dixit and Barry Nalebuff, *The Art of Strategy* (Norton, 2008).
- Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy, Game Theory: [plato.stanford.edu](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/game-theory/) and Prisoner's Dilemma: [plato.stanford.edu](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/prisoner-dilemma/)
- Robert Axelrod, *The Evolution of Cooperation* (1984).
- Thomas Schelling, *The Strategy of Conflict* (1960).
- Colin Camerer, *Behavioral Game Theory* (2003).
