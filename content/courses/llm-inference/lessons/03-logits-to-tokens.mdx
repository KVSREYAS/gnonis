## Softmax

Logits can be any real numbers. **Softmax** turns them into probabilities:

$$p_i = \frac{e^{z_i}}{\sum_j e^{z_j}}$$

Bigger logits get exponentially more probability, and everything sums to 1. In practice you
subtract $\max(z)$ from every logit first: the answer is identical, but $e^{1000}$ no longer
overflows.

**Greedy decoding** skips sampling and just takes the argmax. It is deterministic, but it tends
to repeat itself on long outputs.

## Temperature, top-k, top-p

Samplers reshape the distribution before drawing from it:

- **Temperature $T$** divides every logit by $T$. $T < 1$ sharpens, $T > 1$ flattens, and
  $T \to 0$ becomes greedy.
- **Top-k** keeps only the $k$ most likely tokens.
- **Top-p** (nucleus) keeps the smallest set whose probabilities add up to $p$.

After filtering, the survivors are renormalised and one is sampled at random. Try the knobs below.

## Picking settings for a task

Different products want different sampling.

- **Code, extraction, short factual answers**: low temperature (around 0 to 0.3) or a tight top-p. The best token is usually right, and sampling from the tail invents names and numbers.
- **Creative writing and brainstorming**: temperature around 0.7 to 1.0 with top-p around 0.9. Greedy decoding here tends to repeat safe phrases.
- **Tests that must give the same output every run**: greedy decoding (top-k = 1 or temperature near 0).

Top-p adapts to the shape of the distribution: when the model is sure, the nucleus may hold one or two tokens; when it is unsure, it holds many. Top-k always keeps the same count, which can be too many when the model is sure and too few when it is not.

## Write it yourself

Sampling runs once per generated token, for every sequence in the batch, so serving engines fuse
it into GPU kernels. The maths is small enough to write in a few lines. Implement a numerically
stable softmax with temperature.
