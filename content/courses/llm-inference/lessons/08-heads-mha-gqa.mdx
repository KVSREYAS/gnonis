## Mission briefing

Many small heads beat one big one. Make them carpool on keys and values and your memory bill drops.

**Mission:** Eight crews, one city. Figure out how many getaway cars they actually need.

## Many heads

One attention pattern per layer is limiting. **Multi-head attention (MHA)** splits the
$d_{model}$ dimensions into $h$ heads of size $d_{head} = d_{model} / h$. Each head has its own
$W_Q, W_K, W_V$ slices and its own attention pattern.

In Llama 3 8B, $d_{model} = 4096$ with 32 heads of 128. Heads run in parallel, then their outputs
are concatenated and mixed by an output projection $W_O$.

## Sharing keys and values

At inference time, keys and values are stored for every past token (the KV cache, next module).
Variants cut that cost:

- **MQA** (multi-query): all query heads share a single K/V head.
- **GQA** (grouped-query): query heads are split into groups, with one K/V head per group.

GQA keeps most of MHA's quality at a fraction of the memory. Llama 3 uses it at every size.

## Why it matters for serving

A decode step computes one query per head but reads **all** cached keys and values. With 4x
fewer KV heads, you read 4x fewer bytes per step and can fit 4x more tokens in memory.

Llama 3 70B goes further: 64 query heads, 8 KV heads, a group size of 8.
