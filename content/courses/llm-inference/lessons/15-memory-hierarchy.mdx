## Mission briefing

Registers are your pocket, shared memory your glovebox, HBM the garage across town. Every trip costs time.

**Mission:** Learn the stash spots: what fits in your pocket, your glovebox and the garage across town.

## Two kinds of fast

A GPU has an enormous amount of compute: an H100 SXM does about **989 TFLOP/s** of dense
BF16 matrix maths. Feeding it is the hard part.

Data lives in a hierarchy. **HBM** (high-bandwidth memory) is the big pool: 80 GB at about
**3.35 TB/s**. Close to the compute sits on-chip **SRAM**: registers and shared memory in each of
the 132 SMs, plus a 50 MB L2 cache. SRAM is roughly an order of magnitude faster than HBM, but
measured in kilobytes per SM.

## What lives where

For inference, the big tenants are all in HBM:

- **Weights**: 16 GB for an 8B model in fp16.
- **KV cache**: gigabytes per batch.
- **Activations**: small and short-lived.

A kernel loads tiles from HBM into shared memory and registers, does as much maths on them as it
can, and writes results back. The more work per byte loaded, the less it waits.
