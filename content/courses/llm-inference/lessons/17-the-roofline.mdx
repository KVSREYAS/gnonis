## Two ceilings

The **roofline model** plots attainable performance against arithmetic intensity on log-log axes:

$$P = \min(P_{peak},\; I \times BW)$$

On the left, the sloped roof: you are **memory-bound** and speed equals bandwidth times
intensity. On the right, the flat roof: you are **compute-bound** at peak FLOP/s.

## The ridge point

Where the two roofs meet, $I^* = P_{peak} / BW$. Kernels left of it waste compute, and kernels
right of it waste bandwidth.

| GPU | Peak dense BF16 / FP16 | HBM bandwidth | Ridge |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| L4 | 121 TFLOP/s | 0.3 TB/s | ~400 |
| A100 80GB | 312 TFLOP/s | 2.0 TB/s | ~156 |
| H100 SXM | 989 TFLOP/s | 3.35 TB/s | ~295 |

## Move the dot

## Reading a roofline: a worked example

Take an H100: peak 989 TFLOP/s, bandwidth 3.35 TB/s, ridge ≈ 295 FLOP/byte.

A kernel at 50 FLOP/byte can reach min(989, 50 × 3.35) ≈ 168 TFLOP/s, about 17% of peak. Faster tensor cores would not help it at all; more bandwidth or more reuse per byte would.

A kernel at 1,000 FLOP/byte reaches the full 989. More bandwidth would not help it; only more FLOP/s would.

Two ways to move a kernel right: reuse each loaded byte more (bigger batch, bigger tiles), or make each value smaller (fp8 or int4 weights), which raises FLOPs per byte without changing the maths.
