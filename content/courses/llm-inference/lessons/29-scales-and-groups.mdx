## The scale

Symmetric quantization stretches the integer grid over [−max|w|, +max|w|]. One step is the scale. Each weight rounds to the nearest step, so the error is at most half a step.

## Groups

With one scale per tensor, a single big outlier makes every step huge. Per-channel or per-group scales (GPTQ and AWQ commonly use 128) keep steps small. You pay with extra scale bytes.

Sources: [GPTQ](https://arxiv.org/abs/2210.17323), [AWQ](https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.00978)

## How one outlier wrecks a scale

With symmetric quantization, the scale is max|w| divided by the largest level (127 for int8, 7 for the int4 used here). Any weight smaller than half a step rounds to 0.

Take a row of 4,096 int8 weights, all within ±0.05, plus one outlier of 1.27. The step is 1.27 / 127 = 0.01, so a weight of 0.004 rounds to 0 and 0.026 is stored as 3 (0.03). About a 15% error on a small weight.

With int4 there are far fewer levels, so the same outlier does much more damage. Groups contain it: each group of 128 weights gets its own scale, so the outlier only coarsens its own group, at a cost of one fp16 scale (16 bits) per 128 weights.
