## The scale

Symmetric quantization stretches the integer grid over [−max|w|, +max|w|]. One step is the scale. Each weight rounds to the nearest step, so the error is at most half a step.

## Groups

With one scale per tensor, a single big outlier makes every step huge. Per-channel or per-group scales (GPTQ and AWQ commonly use 128) keep steps small. You pay with extra scale bytes.

Sources: [GPTQ](https://arxiv.org/abs/2210.17323), [AWQ](https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.00978)

## How one outlier wrecks a scale

With symmetric quantization, the scale is chosen so the largest value lands on the top level. The step between levels is that scale, and any value smaller than half a step rounds to zero.

Picture a row of small weights plus one large outlier. The outlier sets the scale, the steps become wide, and most small weights land on zero or on the first level or two: their detail is gone.

int4 has far fewer levels than int8, so the same outlier does much more damage. Groups contain it: each small group of weights gets its own scale, so the outlier only coarsens its own group. The price is one extra scale stored per group, which is why formats balance group size against bits per weight.
