## Why the cache

At long contexts and high concurrency the KV cache dwarfs the weights. Halving its bytes doubles how many sequences fit and halves the KV bytes read per decode step.

## Going lower

FP8 (E4M3) KV is production-ready in vLLM and TensorRT-LLM. KIVI shows 2-bit KV with per-channel keys and per-token values, keeping recent tokens in full precision.

Sources: [vLLM quantized KV cache](https://docs.vllm.ai/en/latest/features/quantization/quantized_kvcache.html), [KIVI](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.02750)

## What KIVI found

KIVI (2024) studied the KV cache's value distributions and found that **keys** have outlier channels, a few fixed channels with large values across tokens, while **values** don't. So it quantizes keys **per channel** (one scale per channel across a group of tokens) and values **per token**.

It also keeps the most recent tokens (a window of about 128) in full precision until a full group can be quantized together.

At 2 bits it reported about 2.6x lower peak memory, up to 4x larger batches and 2.35–3.47x higher throughput on real workloads, with little quality loss.

Source: [KIVI](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.02750)
