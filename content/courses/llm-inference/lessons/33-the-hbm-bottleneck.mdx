## The N² matrices

Naive attention runs as separate kernels, and each one writes its N×N result to HBM for the next to read. With 4k tokens and 32 heads that's a GiB per matrix per layer.

## IO, not FLOPs

Dao et al. counted HBM reads and writes. For standard attention they grow like N·d + N² (the N² is the score matrix). For FlashAttention they grow like N²·d² / M, where M is the SRAM size per SM. With d = 128 and M around 100 KB, d²/M is well below 1, so FlashAttention moves many times fewer bytes. The FLOPs are the same.

Source: [FlashAttention](https://arxiv.org/abs/2205.14135)

## Why the score matrices dominate traffic

At long contexts, one head's score matrix S is enormous: it has a row and a column for every token. The probability matrix P after softmax is just as big.

Naive attention writes S to HBM, reads it back for the mask and softmax, writes P, and reads P again for the multiplication by V. Each of these passes moves a huge matrix while doing only a few operations per element, so the work is memory-bound.

Meanwhile the output O has only one row per token, a tiny fraction of the size of S. Almost all of the traffic is intermediate data that is thrown away. FlashAttention's whole idea is to stop that intermediate data from ever leaving the chip.
