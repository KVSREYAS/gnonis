## Guess, then check

A small draft model proposes several tokens. The big target model scores all of them in **one** forward
pass, which costs about the same as one decode step because decode is memory-bound.

## The accept rule

Verification checks the drafts in order. With greedy decoding, draft token i is accepted if it equals the target's top choice at that position. With sampling, it is accepted with probability min(1, p(x) / q(x)), where p is the target's probability for that token and q the draft's. At the first rejection, the target samples a replacement from the leftover distribution max(0, p − q), normalised, and the remaining drafts are thrown away.

If every draft is accepted, the target's prediction at the last position is still unused, so it gives one extra token for free: the **bonus token**. One target pass therefore yields between 1 and γ + 1 tokens.

## How many tokens per pass?

Verification stops at the first rejection, so a draft only counts if every draft before it was
accepted. The first draft survives often; the second needs two acceptances in a row; deep drafts need
long unbroken runs. So each extra draft adds less than the one before, and how much drafting pays off
depends almost entirely on the acceptance rate, α. The concept page has the exact formula for anyone
who wants it.

## When it backfires

Speculation spends spare compute to save memory-bound steps.

## What the papers measured

Leviathan et al. (2022) reported 2–3x faster generation on T5-XXL with outputs identical to the target model. Chen et al. (2023) showed similar gains with a 70B-class model.

Later methods drop the separate draft model. Medusa (2024) reported over 2.2x with Medusa-1 (heads only) and 2.3–2.8x with Medusa-2 (heads trained with the model). EAGLE (2024) reported 2.7–3.5x on LLaMA2-Chat 70B, keeping the target's output distribution.

All of these numbers are for small batches. The speedup comes from compute that is sitting idle during memory-bound decode. When the batch is large enough to keep that compute busy, the gain shrinks or turns into a loss.

## Variants

Variants worth knowing: Medusa (extra decoding heads), EAGLE (feature-level drafting) and prompt-lookup
or n-gram drafting.

Sources: [Leviathan et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2211.17192), [Chen et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.01318), [Medusa](https://arxiv.org/abs/2401.10774), [EAGLE](https://arxiv.org/abs/2401.15077)
