## Tokens per pass

If each drafted token is accepted with probability α and you draft γ, the target emits on average (1 − α^(γ+1)) / (1 − α) tokens per pass, counting the correction token.

## What controls α

α is how often the draft's token survives the accept rule. It depends on three things:

- **How well the draft matches the target.** A draft distilled on the target's own outputs agrees with it much more often than an off-the-shelf small model does.
- **How predictable the text is.** Code, structured output and quoted material score high α. Creative writing at high temperature scores low.
- **Sampling settings.** With sampling, the chance of acceptance is min(1, p/q) per token, so a draft that is overconfident where the target is unsure gets rejected more often.

The draft must use the same tokenizer as the target, or its tokens can't be checked position by position.

## The cost of drafting

Drafting γ tokens costs γc target-passes of time, where c is the time of one draft step divided by the time of one target step. One iteration therefore costs γc + 1 target-passes and yields (1 − α^(γ+1)) / (1 − α) tokens. The speedup is the ratio of the two. Larger γ helps only while α^γ is still meaningful.

**Distilling** a draft means training the small model to imitate the target's output probabilities. Its guesses then agree with the target more often, which raises α.

Source: [Leviathan et al. 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2211.17192)
