## Total vs active

A router (a small linear layer with a softmax) scores every expert for each token, picks the top-k, and adds their outputs weighted by those scores. Mixtral uses 8 experts with top-2. DeepSeek-V3 uses 256 routed experts plus 1 shared, with 8 active. You need memory for the total and bandwidth for the active.

## MoE at today's scale

Mixtral 8x7B routes each token to 2 of 8 experts per layer: about 47B parameters in total, about 13B active. DeepSeek-V3 goes much further: 671B parameters in total, about 37B active per token.

The serving consequence is the same at both scales. Memory must hold every expert, because any token may pick any of them. Compute and bytes read per token follow the active count, at least at small batch.

At DeepSeek-V3's scale, the weights alone (about 671 GB in fp8) need a multi-GPU node, so expert parallelism places different experts on different GPUs, and tokens are sent to the GPU that holds their chosen expert.

Sources: [Mixtral](https://arxiv.org/abs/2401.04088), [DeepSeek-V3](https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.19437)

## Batches touch more experts

At batch 1 only k experts are read. At larger batches, different tokens pick different experts, and soon nearly all are read every step.

## Expert parallelism

EP spreads experts across GPUs. Tokens are dispatched all-to-all to their experts and combined afterwards. Load balance becomes the headache.

Sources: [Mixtral](https://arxiv.org/abs/2401.04088), [DeepSeek-V3](https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.19437)
