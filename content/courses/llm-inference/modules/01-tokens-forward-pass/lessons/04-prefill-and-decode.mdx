## Mission briefing

Prefill is the planning meeting: the whole prompt at once. Decode is the chase: one token per step, forever.

**Mission:** Plan the heist in one go, then drive the getaway one turn at a time without crashing.

## Two phases

Generating text is a loop, but the first iteration is special.

**Prefill.** The whole prompt goes through the model in one forward pass. All positions are
processed in parallel as big matrix-matrix multiplies. The logits at the last position give the
first output token.

**Decode.** Now the model produces one token per forward pass. Each step feeds in just the newest
token and gets back the next one. This continues until an end-of-sequence token or a length limit.

## The two latency numbers

Users feel two different delays:

- **TTFT** (time to first token): how long prefill takes, plus any queueing.
- **TPOT** (time per output token), also called inter-token latency: the gap between streamed
  tokens during decode.

Total time for an answer of $N$ tokens is roughly:

$$T \approx \text{TTFT} + (N - 1) \times \text{TPOT}$$

## Different bottlenecks

Prefill of 2,000 prompt tokens loads each weight once and uses it 2,000 times. That is plenty of
maths per byte, so the GPU's compute units are the limit.

Decode at batch size 1 loads **every weight** to process **one token**. The tensor cores sit mostly
idle, waiting on memory. That one fact drives batching, quantization and speculative decoding,
which you will meet later.
